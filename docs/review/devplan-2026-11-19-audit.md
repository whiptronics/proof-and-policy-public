### Release reconciliation (plan section 1)

Search engines showed 0.4.0 while the public repository said 0.5.1 because of **stale deployment**, not a mismatch.
0.5.1 was tagged and pushed on 2026-11-15. Its GitHub release files are the maintainer's step, and the website push

## Architecture (plan section 1)

- **Application and routing.** There is one single-page React app (Vite and Recharts) in `web/`. Views are `?v=<view>`
  on `/`, listed in `web/src/lib/url.ts` (`VIEWS`) and grouped in `web/src/lib/nav.ts`. The website (private repository)
  overlays its own frame (`site/src/site-main.tsx`, `site.css`, `index.html`) on the public tag pinned in
  `research.lock.json`. `scripts/build.mjs` builds it, `scripts/prerender.mjs` writes `/read/<view>/` text pages from
  `dist/` with headless Edge, and `scripts/check.mjs` checks every file. Hosting is **Cloudflare Workers static assets**
  (`wrangler.jsonc`, no Worker code), deployed by Workers Builds on every push to `main`. It is not Cloudflare Pages, as
  the plan says. It is static either way, with no running cost.
- **Revenue model** (TypeScript, `web/src/lib`, one engine):
  - `scenario.ts`: the engine.
  - `arrangements.ts`: DABS wholesale, private supply with a charge, earmarking; `solveLever` for break-evens.
  - `wineAlternatives.ts`: 13 named designs; fixed volume first, then demand cases.
  - `wineAccounting.ts`: one price stack per $1 of today's shelf price, plus an incremental ledger that must equal the
    engine's net change.
  - `producerDirect.ts`: scenario D, producer-direct supply, with its delivery cost × premium grid.
  - `wineFlows.ts` and `wineFlowScenarios.ts`: five origins of grocery wine, a straight-line "convenience" channel
    choice, substitution at constant alcohol, and break-evens.
  - `winePriceShare.ts`: price × share grids.
  - `bottle.ts`: one bottle from landed cost to shelf, and DABS's FY2025 accounts from sales to net profit, with the
    statement identities checked.
  - Also `substitution.ts`, `costEvidence.ts`, `wineMargin.ts` (the statutory markup), `briefing.ts` and
    `evidence.ts` (six evidence classes).
- **Datasets and source registries.** 33 source definitions (`config/sources/*.yaml`) are pinned by hash. Each has a
  license in `metadata/rights_registry.csv`, and `metadata/source_registry.csv` is regenerated by
  `alcohol-data sources audit`. Hand-entered values are in `data/curated/manual_observations.csv`: 595 values, **none
  reviewed by a person**. Release validation warns about that on every run.
- **Visualizations.** All charts are Recharts inside `components/ChartFigure.tsx`: title, units, settings, how to read
  it, what it does not establish, sources, release and method versions, a numbers table, and PNG/SVG/CSV downloads.
  The graph package (`scripts/grocery_wine_*graphs.py`) draws PNG/PDF with ParaView's pvpython.
- **Legal history and policy coding.** `src/alcohol_data/legal/*`: the Utah historical-code ledger (verified from
  2003-05-05 for the grocery-wine prohibition), the version ledger for other states, 179 current-law snapshots, and the
  policy catalog of 28 dimensions.
- **Tests.**
  - pytest: 423.
  - vitest: 483 in 38 files.
  - Independent Python recomputations: `grocery_wine_alternatives_check.py`, `grocery_wine_flows_check.py`,
    `grocery_wine_briefing_example.py`, `grocery_wine_robustness_check.py`.
  - `scripts/verify.bat` (21 steps).
  - The website's `check.mjs`.
- **Public downloads and report generators.**
  - The release package: csv.zip, parquet.zip, duckdb, manifest, checksums and notes, as GitHub release assets
    reached through `/release/*`.
  - `npm --prefix web run export:grocery-wine`, which writes JSON and CSV under `data/processed/grocery-wine`.
  - `grocery_wine_bundle_docs.py`: dictionary, register and dataset list.
  - The briefing documents in `docs/briefings/utah-grocery-wine/`. `brief.pdf` is the 2026-10-17 text, because
    reportlab is not installed, and it is excluded from the public repository.
- **Public and private parts.** `scripts/export_public.py` applies `release/public-exclude.txt` (the brief, the review
  packet, the email draft, this log, hosting files) and scans for private text. The website adds only its frame and
  data.
- **AI disclosure and verification metadata.** `lib/disclosure.ts` is used in the footer, in every download and on the
  Methods page. Review status ("not reviewed by a person") is kept separate from the evidence class.
  `machine_assisted_review` is never promoted.

## Preexisting problems found

1. **Headline savings (plan 2.4).** The start page, briefing and brief lead with results that count $1.03M a year of
   allocated store costs as saved. Every page also states the no-savings figure, but lower down. That is the reverse
   of the plan's rule, so the headline changes to no savings.
2. **The 30% shift is price-blind in the alternatives.** The designs that keep revenue whole price grocery wine 39% and
   51% above the state store, and still assume 30% of household purchases move. MISSING_CAPABILITIES already says so,
   and the flows model has a straight-line channel choice, but the headline designs never use it. The design
   comparison applies it.
3. **The engine's group letters differ from the plan's.** The engine's A and B are both DABS supply (the plan's B), and
   its C is the plan's C. A crosswalk is needed, not a rename: ids are in exports, file names and tests.
4. **Two documents disagree with the model's current framing.** brief.pdf is the 2026-10-17 text (reportlab absent),
   and brief.md is private. The new two-page briefing is generated from the model and supersedes them as the printable
   output. The old files are left in place.
5. **Hosting named as Pages in the plan.** It is Workers static assets. This is recorded, and nothing changes.
6. **Not a problem, recorded for the plan's § 5:** the DABS statement conflicts in docs/fiscal-reconciliation.md
   (FY2022, FY2024 and FY2025 totals; p. 32 other income) touch no grocery-wine result, and stay unresolved and shown.

## Implementation plan (this session)

The plan's stages are done in order, inside one branch per repository. Commits are local only.

1. **Stage 2, economics.** Add a new module `web/src/lib/policyDesigns.ts` (`policy-designs@1.0`). It adds no second
   engine: it reads the alternatives and producer-direct results through `evaluateFixed`, `evaluate` and `directDef`.
   - Five arrangements, A to E, with the crosswalk to the engine's ids.
   - Every result in three savings cases: no savings, the headline (as the plan requires); the 2.28¢ allocation; and
     the longer-run case with wages.
   - Bounded channel choice (the flows' straight-line rule at 15%, 30% and 60%), applied to each design's own premium.
   - Break-even conditions per design.
   - A closed-form revenue-neutrality frontier, checked against the engine.
   - The hybrid, built from its parts.
   - A criteria matrix, Pareto set and classification, with configurable thresholds whose defaults are stated and
     never tuned to a result.
   - Tests: the § 15.2 properties, closed forms against the engine, and the plan's zero and edge cases (§ 15.1).
   - The prior models are untouched: the 13 alternatives, the flows and the grids must export byte-identical apart from
     the commit stamp. That is the regression test and the rollback path; the new module can be removed alone.
2. **Stage 3, research.**
   - The Tennessee AJAE article from its public abstract.
   - Colorado's 2023 change from official sources.
   - Pennsylvania and the control states from the saved snapshots.
   - The Community Guide's outlet-density finding.
   - Current Utah off-premise requirements, read from the official code before any safeguard is called new.
   - Federal permit and trade-practice provisions read from the U.S. Code, which closes D's "not read" note.
   - Each item gets its evidence class, URL and access date. A source that cannot be read is recorded, not guessed.
3. **Stage 4, framework.** The statutory change matrix, the framework, the phased option and the stakeholder
   impacts, as documents and as pages.
4. **Stage 5, site.**
   - New views: `designs` (Policy design comparison with Vis 1–5 and business impacts), `states` (What happened in
     other states?), `safeguards` (Public health & safeguards), `framework` (Proposed Utah framework) and `questions`
     (Hard questions).
   - The plan's five navigation groups, and the homepage hero, calls to action and sections A–H.
   - The website: sitemap entries and text pages for the new views. They build only against a research release, so
     the website commit waits for one.
5. **Stage 6, deliverables.** The two-minute briefing on the new headline; a two-page legislative briefing generated
   from the model into HTML and printed to PDF with headless Edge; appendix sections; source-linked tables in the
   export.
6. **Stages 7–8.** verify.bat; a new step for the Python check of the designs; a scratch website build with text pages;
   the overflow sweep at 320/375 px with doubled text; a PDF page count; the verification report; the CHANGELOG
   Unreleased entry; the progress log.
