# Nationwide expansion plan

Requested by Ethan on 2026-10-07. The project grows from a Utah grocery-wine study into a reusable,
nationwide alcohol-policy and outcomes resource. Utah stays the first motivation and Tennessee 2016 stays
a worked example. The database is not organized around either one, or around any preferred outcome.

Work is incremental, on top of the existing pipeline and dashboard. It stays local: nothing is deployed
or published.

The resource has to be able to show benefits, harms, mixed results or insufficient evidence. It must
never be built to reach one conclusion.

## Toward a policy simulator (Ethan, 2026-10-10)

The long-term goal: describe a proposal, such as "Utah adds a tax on wine bottles", and estimate the
changes in prices, purchases, substitution and net government revenue, with documented assumptions and
meaningful uncertainty. It is built in small, usable milestones on top of the steps above, one milestone
per run, each finishing at a stable local checkpoint. Health-outcome forecasting stays out of the
simulator for now. The order is Ethan's.

## Rules that carry over

- A status is recorded for the days its source supports. Current law is never stretched backward; earlier
  days stay unknown.
- A snapshot (a dated print or a current-law page) is not an interval. An interval needs evidence of
  continuity.
- Machine checks and Claude's reading are `machine_assisted_review`, never human review.
- Statewide permission and local implementation are separate dimensions.
- A retail system is never inferred from whether a revenue value exists.
- Blocked hosts are not bypassed. They are recorded with the exact manual step.
- Series breaks stay visible. Definitions that change become separate measures, or separate comparability
  groups, and are never joined into one trend.
